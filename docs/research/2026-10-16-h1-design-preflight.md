## Material finding

### Medium — a valid numeric design can select one independent teacher and produce degenerate confirmatory uncertainty

Evidence: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-16-selective-tree-neurons-design.md:214` defines `K=ceil((z_0.975+z_0.8)^2*s_D^2/delta_plan^2)` without an independent-unit floor. `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-16-selective-tree-neurons-evidence.md:216`–`:217` explicitly tests only `K>=1`. Yet the required final analysis is a whole-teacher bootstrap plus Student-t sensitivity (`spec:222`; `evidence:250`, `:261`).

Concrete admitted case: a resolved pilot mean of 0.1 gives `delta_plan=0.05`; an upper SD bound of 0.01 gives `K=ceil(0.314)=1`. A single observed teacher bootstraps to `[D,D]`; its Student-t interval is undefined. For positive D, the stated positive-interval interpretation can therefore appear to support a population placement benefit without usable independent-teacher uncertainty. Extra optimizer/allocation repetitions cannot repair this because they are explicitly averaged within the sole independent unit.

Minimal resolution: before implementing/accepting the Task 8 decision and freeze validators, declare and enforce an independent-teacher minimum compatible with the frozen interval method, at least rejecting `K<2`; validate finite-sample inference/power for the selected count rather than accepting the normal-approximation count alone. Add a pure design fixture for the above low-SD/large-effect case and a primary-analysis rejection fixture for one independent teacher. This does not require another hypothesis or a practical utility threshold.

## Follow-up assessment: proposed pilot-based confirmation floor

The proposed amendment is technically coherent and a small conservative resolution for this plan: retain the existing normal-approximation `K_calc`, then set `K=max(K_calc, K_pilot_completed)`, validating that `K_pilot_completed` is the accepted variance-calibration cohort's number of independent teachers and is at least eight. This reuses the plan's already-required calibration count (`spec:212`) and its existing fresh-cohort matching rule for unresolved effects (`spec:216`), rather than claiming a new universal sample-size threshold. Confirmation still uses fresh teachers; the pilot contributes the design count, not confirmatory observations. The floor is a new explicit conservative design choice, not a theorem implied by calibration.

Keep the explicit rejection of `K<2`, nonfinite/undefined uncertainty, and zero/undefined teacher-variance cases as superiority-capable inference; route unsupported cases through the existing equivalence/estimation or resource-limited branch. Clarify that a nondegenerate interval from two teachers is only numerically estimable. Neither two, eight, nor the completed pilot count guarantees nominal 95% coverage or sufficient bootstrap accuracy. The frozen finite-sample/resampling diagnostic must be reported with its assumptions and limitations; where it does not support the intended inference, use the existing estimation/resource branch rather than treating the floor as validation.

Recompute the total cohort runtime and storage forecast using the floored count, including repetitions, all required anchors, retries and retained artifacts, and disclose any increase over `K_calc`. Infeasibility invokes the existing pre-confirmation resource decision; do not silently reduce the count or keep a powered-superiority label. A long pilot may make this floor expensive, but that cost is visible and has an existing decision path.

A smaller textual change could reject singleton designs and defer every small-sample design to the existing diagnostic/resource decision. That repairs the immediate undefined/degenerate calculation but leaves the next executor to choose the acceptable independent-unit count. The proposed pilot-based floor is therefore preferable for this plan's executable numeric handoff; it closes that choice with an existing study-specific count while preserving explicit limits on coverage claims.
